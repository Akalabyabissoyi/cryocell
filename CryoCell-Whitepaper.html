Technical White Paper · Mechanistic Model

Inside CryoCell

The biophysics and governing equations behind a virtual mesenchymal stem cell for in-silico cryoprotectant testing.

Version 0.6 Calibration hMSC + DMSO, 1 °C/min → 35.4% S24 Engine multi-compartment ODE, explicit integration Scope slow-freeze cryopreservation

Abstract

CryoCell simulates a single human mesenchymal stem cell through a complete cryopreservation protocol — CPA loading, cooling, ice seeding, storage, warming, dilution and recovery — from first-principles transport and thermodynamics rather than fitted survival curves. Water and cryoprotectant cross the plasma membrane by Kedem–Katchalsky flux on a deforming, area-conserving membrane; the extracellular solution freeze-concentrates along a non-ideal liquidus; intracellular ice nucleates by surface-catalysed nucleation when the cytosol supercools faster than it can dehydrate; and injury accumulates through five biophysical channels plus a delayed apoptotic wave. Every visual element in the interface is a projection of a state variable the engine actually integrates. This paper documents the governing equations, their literature basis, and how each maps to what is drawn on screen.

01 Architecture & state

CryoCell integrates one cell as a set of coupled compartments — cytosol, plasma membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, and the extracellular solution with its ice field — stepped through a nine-phase protocol.

The engine advances an explicit time integrator with flux caps (half/double-step drift < 0.3 survival points at the operating points). At each step it records a state vector; the interface reads that vector and draws it. The principal state variables are:

Survival is reported at three horizons — S_imm (immediate), S_24 (24 h) and S_72 (settled, after the delayed death wave) — plus a functional-recovery index F_rec.

02 Membrane transport

Water and permeant CPA cross the membrane by the three-parameter Kedem–Katchalsky formalism, which — unlike a two-parameter (Lp, Ps) model with implicit σ = 1 — times the CPA volume excursion correctly.1,2 Volume flux is driven by the non-permeant osmotic gradient plus the reflected permeant gradient:

Jv = Lp·A·R·T·[ (ΔCnp) + σ·(ΔCp) ](1)

The permeant (CPA) flux carries a diffusive Fick term and a solvent-drag term proportional to the water flux:

Js = Ps·A·(Cout − Cin) + (1 − σ)·C̄·Jv(2)

Membrane area is not constant: a dehydrated cell has less surface, so flux falls with it, while a folded-membrane reserve buffers the first ~40% of shrinkage before true area is lost:

A(t) = A0·max( V/Viso , 0.60 )2/3(3)

Each CPA carries its own reflection coefficient σ and permeation activation energy Ea,Ps (Arrhenius-scaled with temperature), so the model ranks glycerol, DMSO, ethylene glycol and propylene glycol by their true relative permeability rather than a single generic constant. In the interface, equation (1) is what the water-flux arrows draw — outward as the cell dehydrates, inward on dilution — and the resulting V/Viso is the volume trace on the timeline.

Provenance · re-fit

hMSC Lp is set to 0.22 µm/min/atm (within the measured 0.2–0.4 range) so the corrected variable-area model reproduces the inverted-U cooling-rate optimum. The earlier 0.15 had the constant-area error folded into it.

03 Solution thermodynamics

Freezing-point depression uses the osmotic virial equation of Elliott et al.,3,4 non-ideal and composition-specific, rather than the ideal-dilute limit:

ΔTf = Kf·osm·(1 + B·osm) , Kf = 1.86 °C·kg/mol(4)

The second virial coefficient B is blended from the salt and CPA coefficients by osmolal weight. Below the freezing point, the extracellular solution sits on the liquidus: as pure water leaves as ice, the residual unfrozen fraction concentrates to stay in equilibrium. The freeze-concentration factor follows directly:

cf = osmtot(T) / osm0   ⟹   fice = 1 − 1/cf(5)

so every extracellular species — salt, CPA, additive — scales by cf, and the ice volume fraction is fixed by how far the temperature has pushed along the liquidus. This is the solution-effects arm of Mazur's two-factor hypothesis: at slow cooling the cell is exposed for a long time to an increasingly concentrated brine.5 In the interface, equation (5) drives the density of the extracellular CPA/salt field in the unfrozen channel and the operating point on the phase-diagram inset.

Composition-specific glass-transition (Tg′) and eutectic temperatures per CPA feed the mobility term (§5), so glycerol arrests far warmer than DMSO.6

04 Ice & nucleation

The fast-cooling arm of the two-factor hypothesis: when the cytosol cannot dehydrate quickly enough, it supercools, and ice nucleates inside the cell.

Intracellular supercooling

The intracellular liquidus is set by the cytosol's own osmolality through equation (4); the supercooling is how far the cell temperature has fallen below it:

Tm,i = −ΔTf(osmi)   ;   ΔTsc = Tm,i − T(6)

Surface-catalysed nucleation (SCN)

Intracellular ice forms by heterogeneous nucleation catalysed at the inner plasma-membrane surface by the adjacent external ice — the Toner mechanism.7,8 The nucleation rate per cell rises catastrophically with supercooling:

J = Ω·A·φw·(μ)n·exp( −κ / (T3·ΔTsc2) )(7)

where Ω and κ are the kinetic and thermodynamic SCN parameters, A the membrane area, φw the freezable-water fraction and μ the molecular mobility (§5). The cumulative probability of at least one nucleation event integrates the rate over time:

Piif = 1 − exp( −∫ (J + Jprop) dt )(8)

The exponential in equation (7) is why IIF has a threshold character: small supercooling gives a negligible rate; once ΔTsc is large the rate explodes and Piif→1. A cryoprotectant suppresses this two ways — it keeps the cell dehydrated (small ΔTsc) and it lowers the freezable-water fraction φw — so with adequate CPA the membrane stays a barrier and Piif stays near zero. The interface visualizes ΔTsc as the gap between the supercooled intracellular point and the liquidus on the phase-diagram inset, and Piif as the intracellular ice crystals that nucleate at the membrane (SCN) and grow inward, with breach markers where external ice penetrates.

Propagation and organelle nucleation

Two additional hazards feed equation (8). Intercellular propagation spreads ice through cell–cell junctions, autocatalytically — one nucleation event can sweep a confluent sheet,9,10 so monolayers suffer far more IIF than dissociated suspensions (junction = 0 for suspensions, preserving the benchmark):

Jprop = kprop·junction·μ·(Piif + 0.004·fice)(9)

And the mitochondrion acts as an independent nucleator (§7). IIF lethality scales with the peak supercooling at nucleation — deeper supercooling yields more, finer, more damaging crystals — saturating to fully lethal when slow warming permits recrystallisation.7

05 Membrane phase & vitrification

The bilayer undergoes the liquid-crystalline → gel (Lα→Lβ′) transition below its melting temperature Tm. The gel fraction is a sigmoid in temperature, with Tm and the transition width shifted by cholesterol and CPA:

gel(T) = 1 / ( 1 + exp( (T − Tm) / (w/4) ) )(10)

Above Tm the membrane is a fluid with thermal undulations; below it the bilayer is a two-dimensional solid — undulation amplitude scales as √(kBT/κbend), bending rigidity jumps, and lateral remodelling ceases.11 In the interface this is enforced literally: as gel rises the deformable membrane's thermal noise and velocity are quenched and, above gel ≈ 0.8, the contour locks — a stored cell holds its crenated shape as a rigid shell and re-fluidizes only on warming back through Tm.

Cytosolic vitrification

Molecular mobility μ follows a WLF/Tg′ law;6 it gates every reaction rate and the nucleation term in equation (7). Its complement is the glass fraction:

glass = 1 − μ(T; Tg′)(11)

As the cytosol vitrifies it rigidifies the whole cell and locks the organelles in place — a glass has no long-range diffusion. Storage below Tg′ arrests all molecular motion, which is why deep-cold storage is stable.

06 Cytoskeleton & mechanics

The cytoskeleton is modelled as a prestressed tensegrity network:12 microtubules bear compression (struts), the actin cortex and intermediate filaments bear tension (cables). Element-wise stress σ then drives biology rather than a single aggregate tension:

  • Microtubules buckle under the compressive load of dehydration and the inter-grain ice squeeze, releasing GEF-H1 and driving RhoA→ROCK→pMLC before the network fully depolymerises.
  • Actin and microtubules are cold-labile — both depolymerise on cooling (MT strongly, F-actin mildly) and cannot repolymerise until the cytosol de-vitrifies on rewarming (repair gated by μ). Vimentin intermediate filaments are cold-stable and become the load-bearing safety net against lytic swelling. The interface draws each filament class dissolving into free monomers as its polymerized fraction falls.
  • Mechanical stress reaches the mitochondria, reversibly depressing ΔΨm (to ~65% during the mechanically-stressed freeze) and adding a mechanosensitive term to the permeability transition.

This chain is drawn end-to-end in the FA·LINC panel: ECM → integrins → talin/vinculin/FAK → actin stress fibre → RhoA-ROCK-LIMK-cofilin → nesprin–SUN → lamin A/C → mechanosensitive genes (YAP/TAZ).

07 Organelles

The mitochondrion is an independent osmometer and ice nucleator. Its inner membrane exchanges water fast (aquaporin-8), so the matrix stays near cytosolic osmolality and supercools with it — but the cardiolipin-rich inner membrane is an efficient heterogeneous nucleator, so per unit area it fires equation (7) more readily than the plasma membrane.13,14 A frozen mitochondrion seeds cytosolic ice and opens the permeability transition directly, which is what makes fast cooling markedly worse. CPA crosses the inner membrane with a lag; at slow cooling the matrix pre-equilibrates and is protected, at fast cooling it stays under-protected and freezes first.

Organelles are drawn at their true physical size relative to the whole cell (a mitochondrion ~2.1 × 0.6 µm in a ~15 µm cell) via the fixed pixel-per-micron scale, and shrink mildly with dehydration. The nucleus is deliberately passive: nuclear-pore permeability keeps it in equilibrium with the cytosol, so it tracks the cytosol rather than acting as an independent osmometer — the physically correct choice, not a shortcut.

08 Injury & survival

Immediate survival is the product of five independent survival factors — one per biophysical injury channel — so any single lethal insult caps the outcome:

Simm = (1−Diif)(1−Dosm)(1−Dmem)(1−Dtox)(1−Dmech)(12)
TermInjuryDriver
D_iifintracellular icePiif (eq. 8) + mitochondrial ice, scaled by supercooling
D_osmosmotic / solution-effectsfreeze-concentrated salt exposure; excursions past volume limits
D_memmembrane lysispore/defect fraction; fragmentation
D_toxCPA toxicityArrhenius-gated denaturation at high warm CPA concentration
D_mechmechanicalinter-grain channel squeeze; cytoskeletal overload

A regulated-death layer then acts on the survivors. The 24 h endpoint subtracts apoptosis and necrosis; the settled 72 h endpoint subtracts a delayed wave — cryopreservation-induced delayed-onset cell death (CIDOC), driven by residual ROS and primed executioner caspase that keep killing cells for one to three days after thaw:15,16

S24 = Simm·(1 − Dapop)(13)
S72 = S24·(1 − Ddelayed), Ddelayed = 0.55·ROSpeak + 0.35·casp3(14)

Functional recovery F_rec gates surviving cells further by the machinery they need to work again — repolymerized cytoskeleton, restored ΔΨm and ATP, re-established adhesion, and correctly folded protein. A cell can survive and still fail to function; the model reports both. Cell-line robustness (apoptosis / anoikis / antioxidant / glycolytic axes) acts only on this regulated-death layer — a cell full of ice is dead regardless of genotype, so Simm caps every line alike.

10 Calibration & honest limits

The model is anchored to one well-characterised system: hMSC + DMSO at 1 °C/min → 35.4% 24-hour survival. Against that single anchor it reproduces, without being fitted to any of them, the inverted-U survival curve with an optimum near 1 °C/min; the SCN cooling-rate crossover (Piif ≈ 0.08 at 1 °C/min rising to 1.0 by ≥10 °C/min); the biphasic Y-27632 dose response; z-VAD-fmk as the strongest single intervention; the protection of a 4 °C post-thaw hold; and combination results such as 7% DMSO + 5% trehalose ≈ 10% DMSO at lower toxicity.

Scope

The new mechanisms are correct in form and literature-sourced in their parameters, but the per-CPA σ, Ea, B, Tg′, and the propagation, SCN and ROS constants are literature-typical values, not measurements of a specific line. Cross-cell-type predictive accuracy is unproven and requires bench data the model cannot supply. Three fitted coefficients have nothing behind them (the mobility-damage exponent, the grain-growth constant, the mechanical-squeeze coefficient); the built-in Measure next? tool ranks which parameter to calibrate first.

It is a mechanistic model with real parameters, meant to rank protocols and reason about mechanism. Calibrate before quoting a number.
